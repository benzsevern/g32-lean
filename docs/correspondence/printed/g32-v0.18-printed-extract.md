  64  \subsection{Weil classes}
  65  Let $A$ be a complex abelian variety of dimension $2n$ with an action $\iota\colon K\to\End(A)\otimes\Q$ of an imaginary quadratic field $K$, such that the two embeddings of $K$ in $\C$ each occur with multiplicity $n$ on the tangent space. Then the $K$-line
  66  \[
  67  W_K(A)=\textstyle\bigwedge^{2n}_KH^1(A,\Q)\subset H^{2n}(A,\Q)
  68  \]
  69  is a two-dimensional space of Hodge classes of type $(n,n)$, the \emph{Weil classes} of $A$ \cite{Weil}. For a general such $A$ they are not in the ring generated by divisor classes \cite[\S5]{vanGeemen}. A polarization compatible with $\iota$ determines a $K$-Hermitian form $H$ of signature $(n,n)$ on $H_1(A,\Q)$, and its discriminant $\disc H\in\Q^\times/\Nm K^\times$ is an invariant of the polarized rational Hodge structure. The polarized abelian $2n$-folds of Weil type for $K$ with a fixed discriminant form one family, parametrized by a quotient of the Hermitian symmetric domain of $SU(n,n)$, of dimension $n^2$. The class $(-1)^n$ is called \emph{split}.
  70  
  71  Let $K=\Q(\sqrt{-3})$. The rational primes $p\equiv2\pmod3$ are inert in $K$, so a norm from $K$ has even valuation at each of them. For sixfolds the split class is $-1$, and the class $-2$ is not split: $-2$ and $-2/(-1)=2$ have odd valuation at the inert prime $2$, so neither is a norm.

  73  \subsection{What was known}
  74  For abelian fourfolds of Weil type the Weil classes are algebraic for every $K$ and every discriminant (Markman \cite{Markman}). For sixfolds the split case is known: Schoen for $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ \cite{Schoen98}, and Markman for every $K$ \cite{Markman}. The companion papers treat further non-split cases by other routes: \cite{SevernQ3} proves algebraicity for sixfolds of every discriminant for $K=\Q(\sqrt{-3})$, through split eightfolds and Schoen's product argument, and \cite{SevernQi} treats the class $-3$ for $\Q(i)$. In particular Theorem~A below is also a consequence of \cite{SevernQ3}. The present paper gives an independent proof. It uses no eightfolds, no product argument and no boundary degeneration; every cycle it uses comes from Schoen's theorem on cyclic covers, transported by a correspondence; and its three certificates (the character identity, the dominance and the discriminant) are exact. The method needs only a finite group with a suitable representation and an integral character identity, so it applies to class data where the degeneration of \cite{SevernQ3} is not available; Remark~\ref{rem:witt} explains why no degeneration of the $G_{32}$ family reaches the class $-2$.

  94  \emph{The family.} Let $G\subset GL_4(K)$ be the Shephard--Todd group $G_{32}$, of order $155\,520$, generated by the four matrices of Appendix~\ref{app:tuple}, and $\rho$ its natural representation on $K^4$, with character field $K$. Let $\cI$ be the class of elements with eigenvalues $(1,1,-1,-1)$. We consider Galois covers $C\to\PP^1$ with group $G$, branched over thirteen points, with local monodromy in $\mathcal R_1$ at six of them, in $\mathcal R_2$ at six, and in $\cI$ at one. Such a cover has genus $505\,441$, by Riemann--Hurwitz. Without the point in $\cI$ the multiplicity would be four, giving fourfolds. Thirteen points give a family of dimension $13-3=10$, one more than the dimension $9$ of the moduli space.

  96  \emph{Cycles (Section~\ref{sec:supply}).} For a subgroup $H'\le G$ and a character $\chi$ of $H'$ of order $m\ge3$, the curve $C/\ker\chi$ is a cyclic cover of $C/H'$. When its branch exponents pair off as $\alpha,-\alpha$, Schoen proved that the Weil classes of the primitive part of its Jacobian are algebraic \cite{Schoen88}. That primitive part is a factor of $J(C)$, and the centralizer $U$ of $G$ in the symplectic group of $H^1(C)$ acts on its Weil classes through characters of the abelianization of $U$; it acts on the Weil line $\det H^1(A_\rho)_\rho$ through a character $e_\rho$. If $e_\rho$ is an integer combination of the characters of such pairs, a product of the corresponding Schoen classes, contracted against a $U$-invariant class, maps onto the Weil classes of $A_\rho(C)$, and Milne's theorem on Lefschetz classes \cite{Milne} makes the contracting class algebraic. For $G_{32}$ an identity with fifty-one pairs holds (Proposition~\ref{prop:lattice}); it is printed in Appendix~\ref{app:witness}.

  98  \emph{Dominance (Section~\ref{sec:dominance}).} An explicit cover $C_0$ is the pullback of a rigid rank-four Fuchsian system on $\PP^1$ minus four points along a rational function of degree six. At $C_0$ the codifferential of the period map, a $9\times10$ matrix over $\Q$, has rank only $5$. Moving the twelve reflection points of $C_0$ along a Galois-equivariant rational direction and solving the Schlesinger equations as power series in the deformation parameter $\theta$, all Taylor coefficients lie in $\Q$; a $9\times9$ minor of the codifferential has its first nonzero coefficient at $\theta^7$, an exact rational number (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}). So the period map has rank $9$ at nearby covers.

 130  \section{Abelian varieties of Weil type}\label{sec:weil}
 131  %% ------------------------------------------------------------------
 132  
 133  Throughout, $K$ is an imaginary quadratic field and $\vartheta\in K$ a nonzero element with $\bar\vartheta=-\vartheta$; for $K=\Q(\sqrt{-3})$ we take $\vartheta=\delta=\sqrt{-3}$.
 134  
 135  \begin{definition}\label{def:weiltype}
 136  A \emph{polarized abelian $2n$-fold of Weil type for $K$} is a triple $(A,\iota,E)$: a complex abelian variety $A$ of dimension $2n$, an embedding $\iota\colon K\to\End(A)\otimes\Q$ such that both embeddings of $K$ occur with multiplicity $n$ on $T_0A$, and a polarization $E$ (an alternating form on $H_1(A,\Q)$) with $E(\iota(a)x,y)=E(x,\iota(\bar a)y)$.
 137  \end{definition}
 138  
 139  Following van Geemen \cite[Lemma~5.2]{vanGeemen}, the form $H(x,y)=E(x,\iota(\vartheta)y)+\vartheta E(x,y)$ is $K$-Hermitian of signature $(n,n)$ on the $2n$-dimensional $K$-space $H_1(A,\Q)$. Its discriminant $\disc H\in\Q^\times/\Nm K^\times$ does not depend on $\vartheta$, and it is unchanged by a $K$-linear isogeny that multiplies the polarization by a positive rational number, since such a change multiplies $\det H$ by a $2n$-th power of a rational number, which is a norm. By Landherr's theorem \cite[\S5.4]{vanGeemen}, a nondegenerate $K$-Hermitian space is determined up to isometry by its dimension, signature and discriminant; those of signature $(n,n)$ with an $n$-dimensional totally isotropic subspace are those of discriminant $(-1)^n$. We write $SU_H$ for the special unitary group of $(H_1(A,\Q),H)$, an algebraic group over $\Q$.

 141  \subsection{Moduli}\label{subsec:moduli}
 142  Fix a $K$-Hermitian space $(V,H)$ of dimension $2n$, signature $(n,n)$ and discriminant $\fd$, and an $\mathcal O_K$-stable lattice $\Lambda\subset V$ on which the alternating form $\Tr_{K/\Q}(H/2\vartheta)$, scaled by a positive rational, is integral. The space $\cHn$ of $n$-dimensional complex subspaces of $V_\R$ (for the complex structure $\iota(\vartheta)/|\vartheta|$) on which $H$ is positive is the Hermitian symmetric domain of $SU(n,n)$, connected of dimension $n^2$. A point of $\cHn$ determines a Hodge structure of weight one on $V$ of Weil type for $K$, polarized by $\Tr_{K/\Q}(H/2\vartheta)$, and every such polarized Hodge structure arises in this way \cite[5.10 and the proof of 6.11]{vanGeemen}. For $\ell\ge3$ the congruence subgroup $\Gamma(\ell)$ of the unitary group of $(\Lambda,H)$ is torsion free, and $\cM=\Gamma(\ell)\backslash\cHn$ is a smooth connected quasi-projective variety with a universal abelian scheme $f\colon\mathcal A\to\cM$ with $K$-action \cite[\S\S5.5--5.11]{vanGeemen}.
 143  
 144  \begin{lemma}[Isogeny invariance]\label{lem:isogeny}
 145  If $\psi\colon A'\to A$ is a $K$-linear isogeny, then $W_K(A)$ is spanned by algebraic classes if and only if $W_K(A')$ is. Every polarized abelian $2n$-fold of Weil type for $K$ with discriminant $\fd$ is $K$-isogenous to a fibre of $\mathcal A\to\cM$.
 146  \end{lemma}
 147  
 148  \begin{proof}
 149  Pullback and pushforward by $\psi$ are algebraic correspondences, and $\psi^*$ maps $W_K(A)$ isomorphically onto $W_K(A')$, since it is a $K$-linear isomorphism on $H^1(-,\Q)$. For the second statement, Landherr's theorem gives a $K$-isometry from $(H_1(A,\Q),H)$ to $(V,H)$; the image of $H_1(A,\Z)$ is commensurable with $\Lambda$, so an integer multiple of it lies in $\Lambda$, and multiplication by that integer is a $K$-linear isogeny from $A$ to the fibre at the point given by the complex structure of $A$.
 150  \end{proof}

 156  \subsection{Hurwitz spaces}\label{subsec:hurwitz}
 157  Fix a finite group $G$ and conjugacy classes $\mathcal C_1,\dots,\mathcal C_r$ of $G$, $r\ge4$. The \emph{Nielsen class} is the set of tuples $(g_1,\dots,g_r)$ with $g_j\in\mathcal C_j$, $g_1\cdots g_r=1$ and $\langle g_1,\dots,g_r\rangle=G$. By the Riemann existence theorem, a connected Galois cover $C\to\PP^1$ with group $G$, branched over $r$ ordered points $p_1,\dots,p_r$, is the same as such a tuple up to simultaneous conjugation, once standard generators of $\pi_1(\PP^1-\{p_j\})$ are fixed: $g_j$ is the monodromy along a loop around $p_j$. More generally, a homomorphism $\pi_1(\PP^1-\{p_j\})\to G$ that is onto determines a connected Galois $G$-cover. The Hurwitz space $\Hur$ of such covers is finite and \'etale over the configuration space of $r$ distinct ordered points; its connected components are smooth, hence irreducible, of dimension $r-3$ modulo $PGL_2$ \cite{FriedVolklein}.
 158  
 159  For an irreducible complex representation $\tau$ of $G$ write $H^1(C,\C)=\bigoplus_\tau\tau\otimes M_\tau$ and $m_\tau=\dim M_\tau$, with $M_\tau=\Hom_G(\tau,H^1(C,\C))$. Since $G$ acts by morphisms of Hodge structures, $M_\tau$ is a complex vector space with a decomposition $M_\tau=\Hom_G(\tau,H^{1,0})\oplus\Hom_G(\tau,H^{0,1})$; it is not in general defined over $\Q$. For a representation realizable over $K$, as in Subsection~\ref{subsec:pt}, $M_\tau$ is the complexification of a $K$-space $M$ (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:weiltype}) along one embedding of $K$. For $g\in G$ write $c_\tau(g)=\rk(\tau(g)-1)$, and $\age\tau(g)=\sum_k\alpha_k$ when the eigenvalues of $\tau(g)$ are $e^{2\pi i\alpha_k}$ with $0\le\alpha_k<1$.
 160  
 161  \begin{proposition}\label{prop:mult}
 162  If $\tau$ is irreducible and nontrivial of dimension $d$, then
 163  \[
 164  m_\tau=-2d+\sum_{j=1}^rc_\tau(g_j),\qquad \dim\Hom_G(\tau,H^0(C,K_C))=\sum_{j=1}^r\age\tau(g_j^{-1})-d.
 165  \]
 166  \end{proposition}
 167  
 168  \begin{proof}
 169  Let $U_0=\PP^1-\{p_j\}$ and $\mathbb V$ the local system on $U_0$ attached to $\tau$. Then $m_\tau$ is the dimension of the parabolic cohomology $H^1(\PP^1,j_*\mathbb V)$, the image of $H^1_c(U_0,\mathbb V)\to H^1(U_0,\mathbb V)$. Since $\tau$ is irreducible and nontrivial, $H^0(U_0,\mathbb V)=0$, and $H^2(U_0,\mathbb V)=0$ because $U_0$ is an affine curve; so $\dim H^1(U_0,\mathbb V)=(r-2)d$. Likewise $H^0_c=H^2_c=0$, the latter by duality with $H^0(U_0,\mathbb V^\vee)=0$, so $\dim H^1_c(U_0,\mathbb V)=(r-2)d$. The long exact sequence for $U_0$ and small punctured discs $\Delta_j^*$ around the $p_j$ begins
 170  \[
 171  0=H^0(U_0,\mathbb V)\to\textstyle\bigoplus_jH^0(\Delta_j^*,\mathbb V)\to H^1_c(U_0,\mathbb V)\to H^1(U_0,\mathbb V)\to\cdots,
 172  \]
 173  so the image of $H^1_c\to H^1$ has dimension $(r-2)d-\sum_j(d-c_\tau(g_j))$. This gives the first formula. The second is the Chevalley--Weil formula \cite[Theorem~2.10]{FGP}.
 174  \end{proof}

 176  \subsection{The Weil-type factor}\label{subsec:pt}
 177  Let $\sigma$ be an irreducible representation of $G$ whose character field is $K$ and which is realizable over $K$, on $V_\sigma=K^d$. The simple factor of $\Q[G]$ attached to $\sigma$ is $\Q[G]\varepsilon\cong\End_K(V_\sigma)\cong M_d(K)$, with centre $K$. Averaging gives a $G$-invariant positive definite $K$-Hermitian form $h_V$ on $V_\sigma$; the Rosati involution $g\mapsto g^{-1}$ acts on $\Q[G]\varepsilon$ as the $h_V$-adjoint and on its centre as complex conjugation.
 178  
 179  \begin{definition}\label{def:pt}
 180  Choose $v\in V_\sigma$ with $h_V(v,v)\ne0$, and let $e\in\Q[G]\varepsilon$ be the $h_V$-orthogonal projection onto the line $Kv$, a primitive idempotent fixed by the Rosati involution. Choose an integer $N_0\ge1$ with $N_0e\in\Z[G]$; then $N_0e$ is an endomorphism of $J(C)$, not itself idempotent. Put
 181  \[
 182  A_\sigma(C)=\operatorname{im}\big(N_0e\colon J(C)\to J(C)\big),
 183  \]
 184  with the restricted polarization $E$ and the action $\iota$ of the centre $K$. It does not depend on $N_0$, and other choices of $v$ give $K$-isogenous abelian varieties.
 185  \end{definition}
 186  
 187  The $\sigma$-isotypic part of $J(C)$ is isogenous to $A_\sigma(C)^d$. Factors of this kind are studied as Prym--Tyurin varieties in \cite[Ch.~12]{BL}.

 189  \begin{lemma}\label{lem:weiltype}
 190  $(A_\sigma(C),\iota,E)$ is a polarized abelian variety of dimension $m_\sigma$, and the two embeddings of $K$ occur on its tangent space with multiplicities the Hodge numbers $(p_\sigma,q_\sigma)$ of $M_\sigma$. If $m_\sigma=2n$ and $(p_\sigma,q_\sigma)=(n,n)$, it is a polarized abelian $2n$-fold of Weil type for $K$.
 191  \end{lemma}
 192  
 193  \begin{proof}
 194  The $\sigma$-isotypic part of $H_1(J(C),\Q)$ is $V_\sigma\otimes_KM$ with $M=\Hom_{K[G]}(V_\sigma,H_1(J(C),\Q)_\varepsilon)$, and $H_1(A_\sigma,\Q)=Kv\otimes_KM\cong M$. After extending scalars along the two embeddings of $K$, $M$ becomes $M_\sigma$ and $M_{\bar\sigma}$. The Rosati involution of $E$ restricts on $\iota(K)$ to that of $\Q[G]$ on the centre, which is complex conjugation.
 195  \end{proof}

 197  \begin{lemma}\label{lem:constancy}
 198  On a connected component $S$ of $\Hur$, the discriminant of the Hermitian form of $A_\sigma(C)$ does not depend on $C\in S$.
 199  \end{lemma}

 202  The spaces $H_1(A_\sigma(C),\Q)$ with $\iota$ and $E$ form a local system over $S$, since $e$ and $\iota$ act by flat endomorphisms and $E$ is flat; parallel transport is a $K$-isometry.

 205  \subsection{The group $G_{32}$ and the class datum}\label{subsec:g32}
 206  Let $K=\Q(\sqrt{-3})$, and let $G\subset GL_4(K)$ be the group generated by the matrices $r_1,r_2,x,y$ of Appendix~\ref{app:tuple}. It has order $155\,520$, and it is the Shephard--Todd group $G_{32}$; we use only the following properties, which are checked in Appendix~\ref{app:tuple}. Its natural representation $\rho$ on $V_\rho=K^4$ is absolutely irreducible, so its character field is $K$ and it is realizable over $K$. The matrices preserve the standard Hermitian form, which is therefore $h_V$. The centre $Z(G)$ has order six, $G/Z(G)\cong PSp_4(3)$ is simple of order $25\,920$, the derived group of $G$ is the kernel of $\det\rho$, of index three, and every irreducible character of $G$ takes values in $K$. Let $\mathcal R_1$ and $\mathcal R_2$ be the classes of the reflections with nontrivial eigenvalue $\omega$ and $\omega^2$ respectively; they are the classes of $r_1$ and $r_2$, and they consist of mutually inverse elements. Let $\cI$ be the class of $x^2$, whose eigenvalues are $(1,1,-1,-1)$.
 207  
 208  \begin{definition}\label{def:datum}
 209  Let $\Hur$ be the Hurwitz space of connected Galois covers $C\to\PP^1$ with group $G$, branched over thirteen points, with local monodromy in $\mathcal R_1$ at six of them, in $\mathcal R_2$ at six, and in $\cI$ at one. We call this list of classes the \emph{class datum}, and write $\Hur^\circ$ for the connected component of $\Hur$ that contains the cover $C_0$ constructed in Lemma~\ref{lem:surj}.
 210  \end{definition}

 212  \begin{lemma}\label{lem:sixfold}
 213  For every $C\in\Hur$, $\rho$ occurs in $H^1(C)$ with multiplicity $6$ and Hodge numbers $(3,3)$, so $A_\rho(C)$ is a polarized abelian sixfold of Weil type for $K$. The genus of $C$ is $505\,441$.
 214  \end{lemma}
 215  
 216  \begin{proof}
 217  A reflection has $c_\rho=1$, and an element of $\cI$ has $c_\rho=2$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:mult} gives $m_\rho=-8+12+2=6$. The inverse of an element of $\mathcal R_1$ has eigenvalues $(\omega^2,1,1,1)$, of age $\tfrac23$; that of an element of $\mathcal R_2$ has age $\tfrac13$; an element of $\cI$ has age $1$. So $\dim\Hom_G(\rho,H^0(K_C))=6\cdot\tfrac23+6\cdot\tfrac13+1-4=3$, and the Hodge numbers are $(3,3)$. Conclude with Lemma~\ref{lem:weiltype}. Riemann--Hurwitz gives $2g-2=155\,520\,\big(-2+12\cdot\tfrac23+\tfrac12\big)=1\,010\,880$.
 218  \end{proof}

 727  \begin{lemma}\label{lem:sl}
 728  $\Lambda^{SL(\mathsf W)}$ is generated by the $\Theta(u,u')$, the $\det(\mathsf W\otimes x)$ and the $\det(\mathsf W^*\otimes x')$.

 730  
 731  \begin{proof}
 732  We deduce it from the first fundamental theorem for $SL(\mathsf W)$, in the form of Kraft and Procesi \cite[\S8.4, Theorem (FFT for $SL_n$), p.~90]{KraftProcesi}: \emph{the ring of invariants $K[V^p\oplus V^{*q}]^{SL(V)}$ is generated by the scalar products $\langle j\mid i\rangle$ and the determinants $[i_1,\dots,i_n]$ and $[j_1,\dots,j_n]^*$}, for a vector space $V$ of dimension $n$ over a field of characteristic zero. An $SL(\mathsf W)$-invariant element of $\mathsf W^{\otimes p}\otimes\mathsf W^{*\otimes q}$ is an invariant polynomial on $\mathsf W^{*p}\oplus\mathsf W^q$ that is homogeneous of degree one in each of the $p+q$ vector variables; apply the theorem with $V=\mathsf W^*$. A polynomial in the scalar products and determinants that is of degree one in each variable is a linear combination of products in which every variable occurs exactly once. So the invariants in $\mathsf W^{\otimes p}\otimes\mathsf W^{*\otimes q}$ are spanned by tensor products of contractions $\sum_iw_i\otimes w_i^*$ (one factor $\mathsf W$ and one factor $\mathsf W^*$), determinant tensors $\sum_{\pi\in S_N}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\,w_{\pi(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{\pi(N)}$ (in $N$ factors $\mathsf W$), and dual determinant tensors.
